I was a bit skeptical in writing this post here, reasons may be many, but they couldn't surpass the reasons behind writing it nowhere but just here, on my own blog. I'm sure I now know what to write where because of how costly being vulnerable can be and so here comes another train of thought.

I had to visit my village today, situated around 35 Kms from my hometown. I remember I last visited it around 10 yrs ago. But little did I know that I too could have an attachment with a place which I barely visited. In almost all families in Northern part of India, villages are where the roots of somebody's family exist. Mine too: my grandfather used to tell me how our village's name came into existence in a war because of its citizens' bravery, all his childhood times and how he used to get beaten by his teacher, a race in which he once got a soap as first prize, everything about his friends and their funny nicknames, everything about how he started his career in administrative services out of literally nothing and what he now wants from his possible next life. I learnt a lot from him, enjoyed our after dinner conversations: sometimes teaching him what I learnt, and sometimes he amazing me with his persistence and survival instincts.

The last time I visited my village with my grandfather, he was all happy: showing the green farms he has, the silent lives in village, our historical house in which he grew up but which now just stands like a half broken abandoned place. But this time, I saw him sad because of the construction carried out by governement for one of their big projects, taking away homes and lands of all villagers. I saw him helpless, signing those documents, as if someone seeing a self-built mountain crash to its bottom in a fraction of seconds, questioning his own worth, his own life.

I do not know how much of a value this (or any) government's project is, but it is hard for me to imagine people accepting their homes, their lands, their identity, their lives being traded for some amount of money. 

I don't think my grandpa will take too long to accept the truth but it makes one question: what do we consider as an accomplishment of our lives? Do we even have any right to question it or is *life's accomplishment* just something bound within law and order, within our own ability to consider what lies ahead of the *horizon* or what exactly the *horizon* is? 

I do not really know. But may be courage is all that counts, even if it is a for a matter of few seconds. May be my grandpa will not remember much of his house but more about how he once took some courageous decision that made him who he is today. May be he being able to tell about his struggles to his grandchildren is what no one can ever take away from him. Maybe it all comes to a point where a person is just a collection of stories: some good, some bad that can influence other people in certain direction when he/she is having hard time showing some courage to his/her ownself.

Maybe it all just comes to stories.
